Page metadata is one expression of a wider information management posture. See [Page metadata](/plan/metadata/) for the per-page fields. This page describes where that guidance comes from, and what to do when the inputs your page depends on, like a controlled vocabulary or a parent registry, are not yet in place at your agency.

Where the obligation comes from
-------------------------------

Two named Actions sit above the per-page guidance. They come from [Building trust in the public record](https://www.naa.gov.au/information-management/information-management-policies/building-trust-public-record).

Action 10 requires agencies to adopt the minimum metadata set when creating and managing information assets. Action 11 requires alignment with the Australian Government Recordkeeping Metadata Standard for more comprehensive recordkeeping metadata implementation.

Standards to align with
-----------------------

Two named standards back the field-level guidance for the per-page fields.

The required fields on the Page metadata page are a superset of the Commonwealth's baseline set of metadata fields. See [minimum metadata set](https://www.naa.gov.au/node/862).

Apply the comprehensive recordkeeping metadata standard when your page is part of a record covered by recordkeeping obligations. See [Australian Government Recordkeeping Metadata Standard (AGRkMS)](https://www.naa.gov.au/node/265).

Controlled vocabularies
-----------------------

The `focus-area` field on the Page metadata page takes a value from a controlled vocabulary. Do not invent new focus areas per page. Pull from your agency's published list.

A business glossary lists the approved terms your agency uses to describe its data and its work. Its purpose is to eliminate ambiguity across business areas. See [Developing a business glossary](https://www.naa.gov.au/node/3003). Hooking your page metadata into that glossary means readers, AI systems, and content audits all see the same set of terms.

If your agency does not have a published list of focus areas, that is a metadata governance gap. Flag it to your information management lead before publishing, or use the closest existing term and note the gap on the page.

Building capability
-------------------

Two resources for content owners and information management leads who want to deepen their metadata practice.

The National Archives' published capability framework lists what 'Metadata' capability looks like at each level. See [capabilities for information management professionals](https://www.naa.gov.au/node/2726).

DAMA International's reference text covers the full metadata lifecycle from concept through retirement. See [Data Management Body of Knowledge, chapter 12](https://www.dama.org/cpages/body-of-knowledge).
