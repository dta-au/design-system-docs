If you own content on digital.gov.au, this is your starting point. The design library sets out how pages on the site are named, written, structured and built, so visitors get a consistent experience across every part of digital.gov.au.

What's in the library
---------------------

The library is organised into four collections. Read them in this order on your first visit.

1. **[Foundations](/foundations/)**: the design and accessibility principles every page is built on.
2. **[Components](/components/)**: the building blocks (buttons, cards, callouts, tables) you place on a page.
3. **[Patterns](/patterns/)**: reusable combinations of components that solve common problems.
4. **[Templates](/templates/)**: the purpose, structure, and writing conventions for each content type on digital.gov.au.

Where to start
--------------

1. Read [Foundations](/foundations/) before anything else. The accessibility and visual principles there carry through every other page in the library.
2. Find your content type in [Templates](/templates/). Each template tells you what sections to include, what to call them, and how to write them.
3. Use [Components](/components/) and [Patterns](/patterns/) to build the page. Each entry shows when to reach for the component, and when not to.

What's expected of you
----------------------

As a content owner, you are responsible for:

- the page's title, description and other [metadata](/plan/metadata/)
- the [content](/plan/content/) on the page (what it says, how it's structured, how it's written)
- which template the page follows.

[Development](/plan/development/) decisions (component selection, code, build) are handled by the development team, but you sign off on the result.

Naming pages
------------

The page title is the single most important piece of metadata. It becomes the page's `<h1>`, its listing card heading, and the link text people see when others link to your page from elsewhere.

### Don't

- Use 'Appendix #' in the title of your web content page. Use a meaningful title instead, and update in-text references to link to the page.
